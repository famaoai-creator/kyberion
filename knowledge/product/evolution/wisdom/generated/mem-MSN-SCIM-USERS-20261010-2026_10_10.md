# SCIM provisioning must never outrank the token: role cap and provenance

An IdP-driven SCIM endpoint that can edit identity bindings or status of any member is an account-takeover path. Bound SCIM to members at or below the token role cap, never owners, and track provenance so it only moves bindings and reactivates suspensions it created.

## Hint Scope

mission

## Trigger Phrases

- ## Lesson

When an external provisioner (SCIM, IdP sync) writes into the same member registry that authenticates users, treat every mutable attribute that affects sign-in or lifecycle as an authority boundary, not data:

- **Role cap**: the provisioning token carries a default role; it may mutate only members whose roles in the tenant are non-owner and rank at or below that role. Unknown roles fail closed.
- **Binding provenance**: record `provisioned_by` on identity bindings it creates. It may replace or remove only bindings its own tenant tokens created, and may attach an `externalId` only to members whose every identity it created (or who have none and no access token). Owner re-assertion of a binding strips the provenance.
- **Suspension provenance**: record `suspended_by`; reactivate only what its own tenant tokens suspended; drop the field whenever the member becomes active so no write path leaves it stale.
- **Issuer hygiene**: compare issuers through one normaliser, refuse chat-surface issuers, and refuse tokens for inactive tenants.
- **PUT semantics**: an absent `externalId` on PUT means unchanged; only explicit null/remove unbinds, otherwise IdPs that omit it lock users out.
