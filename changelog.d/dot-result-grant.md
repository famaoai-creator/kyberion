category: Changed
---

- **DOT result storage is authorized by its grant, not by a dedicated role** — the internal `dot_result_writer` role is gone (security policy, authority role index and definition, role-assumption policy, `authority.ts`). The DOT result facade issues the same exact, synchronous, non-transferable grant as before and binds the I/O to the grant's tenant and organization while it is active; tier-guard treats that active grant as the authority for the I/O it covers, after the request ceiling and the tenant-scope check. A caller bound to another tenant is refused. The other dedicated store roles (discussion requests, customer decision invites, task source workspaces, PR publication) are now checked through one table in `tier-guard-store-grants.ts` instead of a role-name branch at each decision; they keep their roles for now.
- **Customer decision invite storage is authorized by its exact capability, not by a dedicated role** — the shared core role `customer_decision_invite_store` is gone (security policy, authority role index and definition, role-assumption policy, `authority.ts`, store catalog `legacy_roles`). The invite facade binds the I/O to the capability's tenant (the caller keeps its own role) and refuses a caller bound to another tenant. tier-guard treats the active exact invite capability as the authority for the one record (or read-only listing) it covers, after the request ceiling, the tenant-scope check and the CD-10 registered-tenant / revoke-only content check, which now applies whenever the capability is active. Broadened, append- or name-prefix-enlarged lookalikes authorize nothing.
- **Discussion request receipts are authorized by an opaque grant, not by a dedicated role** — the receipt facade issues an opaque, synchronous, non-transferable grant (the same issuer mechanism as DOT results) over the two records of one request; forged, copied, escaped, expired and counterfeit-issuer scopes authorize nothing. The `discussion_request_store` role is gone (security policy, authority role index and definition, `authority.ts`, store catalog `legacy_roles`); tier-guard treats the active grant as the authority after the request ceiling and the tenant-scope check, including the structural mkdir of the tenant container. Who may write receipts is unchanged: before issuing, the facade applies the same role-assumption rule to the name `discussion_request_store`, which stays in Concierge's `may_assume` as a grant issuance right, so other surfaces still get `[ROLE_ASSUMPTION_DENIED]`. The facade binds the I/O to the selected tenant (caller keeps its own role, no organization) and refuses a caller bound to another tenant.
- **Private task source workspace I/O is authorized by its window, not by a dedicated role** — the shared core role `task_source_workspace` is gone (security policy, role-assumption policy shared roles, the `authority.ts` assumption gate, the store-grant table entry and the role-keyed narrowing). The window over one live, same-tier task workspace file is now module-private (no `globalThis` registry): a second module instance of `@agent/core` neither sees nor honours it, forged registries and lookalike windows authorize nothing, and it is synchronous (a thenable result is refused, a captured continuation finds no window). Only `workforce/task-source-workspace-io.ts` opens it (import boundary test). tier-guard treats the live window as the authority for its exact target (and mkdir of missing ancestors when writing) inside its physical partition `workspaces/<personal|confidential>/<tenant|shared>/`; every I/O made inside the window stays narrowed to it, as under the role. The caller keeps its own role and persona, bound to the task's tenant and organization as the role assumption bound it. The liveness check (claim, lease, workspace ledger) runs as the caller with the window suspended, so its ledger reads are not narrowed and a stale claim still blocks the I/O. Any in-process caller may still open a window.
- **PR publication store I/O is authorized by its grant, not by a dedicated role** — the shared core role `pr_publication_store` is gone (security policy, authority role index and definition, store catalog `legacy_roles`, the tier-guard role-name branches and the last row of the store-grant table, which is removed with `dedicatedStoreGrantAllows`). Each synchronous store access of a VCS host run (charter read, tenant charter ledger append, review artifact, PR body stage, external-operation reservation; one store each) issues a module-private grant: a second module instance, a captured continuation, a thenable result or a lookalike authorizes nothing, and only the governed runtime, the two VCS hosts, the charter registry and the reservation store may open one (import boundary test; the core barrel no longer re-exports the issuers). tier-guard narrows every I/O inside the grant to its exact ceiling and treats the grant as the authority under the roots the former role's policy covered; ancestors above them stay with the caller's own policy. Who may issue is unchanged: the facade applies the former shared-role assumption rule to the name `pr_publication_store`, which stays in `shared_core_roles` as an issuance right; assuming it as a role is refused. An installation whose policy predates the store gets a grant that only narrows; the installation publication role migration now registers only that issuance right. The caller keeps its role and persona; organization publications bind the binding's tenant and refuse a caller bound to another tenant, person publications stay unbound.
- **Tenant autonomy learning records are authorized by their grant, not by a dedicated role** — the shared core role `autonomy_learning_store` is gone (security policy, `authority.ts` persona, tenant-bound role list and assumption check, store catalog `legacy_roles`, both tier-guard role-name branches). The autonomy action state facade issues an opaque, synchronous grant (the same issuer mechanism as Discussion requests) over the exact ceiling of one tenant partition (outcomes ledger, overrides, their rewrite siblings and compaction archive); structural lookalikes, copies, counterfeit issuers, captured continuations and escaped or asynchronous results authorize nothing, and only `governance/autonomy-action-state.ts` may issue it (import boundary test). tier-guard treats the live grant as the authority inside its ceiling under the store root, after the request ceiling and the tenant-scope check. Who may record is unchanged: before issuing, the facade applies the former shared-role assumption rule to the name `autonomy_learning_store`, which stays in `shared_core_roles` as an issuance right. The caller keeps its role and persona, bound to the record's tenant; a caller bound to another tenant is refused. Former store role names (`pr_publication_store`, `autonomy_learning_store`) are refused as roles, and a process still launched under one gets no I/O.
- **Tenant pipeline run journals are authorized by their grant, not by a dedicated role** — the shared core role `pipeline_journal_store` is gone (security policy, `authority.ts` persona, tenant-bound role list and assumption check, store catalog `legacy_roles`, both tier-guard role-name branches). A caller bound to a tenant still records that tenant's confidential library run journal inside an opaque, synchronous grant over the journal file, its writer lease and lease metrics (lookalikes, copies, counterfeit issuers, captured continuations authorize nothing; only `pipeline/pipeline-run-journal.ts` may issue it). tier-guard treats the live grant as the authority inside its ceiling under the store root. Who may record is unchanged (the former shared-role assumption rule applies to the issuance right `pipeline_journal_store`); the caller keeps its role and persona, bound to the journal's tenant.
- **Allocated credential material is read under its grant, not a dedicated reader role** — the shared core role `credential_material_reader` is gone (security policy, `authority.ts` tenant-bound role list and assumption check, store catalog `legacy_roles`, the tier-guard role-name branch). The allocation broker's opaque, synchronous grant over the one admitted connection file is now the authority for that read (and its ancestors' metadata); only `secret/credential-allocations.ts` may issue it (import boundary test). Who may read is unchanged (the former shared-role assumption rule applies to the issuance right `credential_material_reader`); the caller keeps its role and persona, bound to the allocation's tenant and the claim's mission/task.
- **Memory provenance lineage reads are authorized by their grant, not a dedicated reader role** — the shared core role `memory_provenance_reader` is gone (security policy, `authority.ts` persona and assumption check, store catalog `legacy_roles`, the tier-guard role-name branch). The lineage read binding is now a synchronous module-private grant (it ends when the read returns; a thenable result is refused, a captured continuation finds no grant; only `knowledge/memory-provenance.ts` opens it). While it is live, every I/O is narrowed to the one state file read; it authorizes that read under the roots the former role covered. Who may read is unchanged: the store applies the former shared-role assumption rule to the issuance right `memory_provenance_reader` and the former binding checks (confidential only for the active tenant, personal only for the owner NHI); the caller keeps its role and persona.
- **Person charter store I/O is authorized by its grant, not by a dedicated role** — the shared core role `person_charter_store` is gone (security policy, authority role index and definition, `authority.ts` persona, store catalog `legacy_roles`, the tier-guard role-name branch). Inside an installed owner/NHI binding, each synchronous person charter registry access or canonical personal reservation opens a module-private grant through `governance/person-charter-store-access.ts` (the only issuer; the binding now ends when its run settles and grants are synchronous). tier-guard narrows every I/O inside the grant to its exact resources where the former role was effectively authorized (person charters and the Vitest reservation scratch; the durable personal reservation partition stays refused, as it was under the role) and treats the grant as the authority there. Who may issue is unchanged (the former shared-role assumption rule applies to the issuance right `person_charter_store`); an installation that has not registered it gets a grant that admits nothing, and the publication role migration now registers only that issuance right. The caller keeps its role and persona and runs unbound, as before.
- **Customer decision consumer locks are authorized by their grant, not by a dedicated role** — the shared core role `customer_decision_consumer_store` is gone (security policy, authority role index and definition, `authority.ts` tenant-bound role list and assumption check, store catalog `legacy_roles`, the tier-guard role-name branch). Mission task boundaries and the operator re-ask write open an opaque, synchronous consumer-lock grant through `customer-decision/consumer-grant-access.ts` (the only issuer); lookalikes, copies, counterfeit issuers and captured continuations authorize nothing, and the sweep kind still only counts inside the sweep (its marker is now module-private). tier-guard treats the live grant as the authority inside its ceiling under the tenant decisions root, and every write inside it is still held to the CD-09 content rules, now keyed on the live grant. Who may write is unchanged (the former shared-role assumption rule applies to the issuance right `customer_decision_consumer_store`); the caller keeps its role and persona, bound to the case's tenant.
