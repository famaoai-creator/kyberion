---
category: Changed
---

- **DOT result storage is authorized by its grant, not by a dedicated role** — the internal `dot_result_writer` role is gone (security policy, authority role index and definition, role-assumption policy, `authority.ts`). The DOT result facade issues the same exact, synchronous, non-transferable grant as before and binds the I/O to the grant's tenant and organization while it is active; tier-guard treats that active grant as the authority for the I/O it covers, after the request ceiling and the tenant-scope check. A caller bound to another tenant is refused. The other dedicated store roles (discussion requests, customer decision invites, task source workspaces, PR publication) are now checked through one table in `tier-guard-store-grants.ts` instead of a role-name branch at each decision; they keep their roles for now.
- **Customer decision invite storage is authorized by its exact capability, not by a dedicated role** — the shared core role `customer_decision_invite_store` is gone (security policy, authority role index and definition, role-assumption policy, `authority.ts`, store catalog `legacy_roles`). The invite facade binds the I/O to the capability's tenant (the caller keeps its own role) and refuses a caller bound to another tenant. tier-guard treats the active exact invite capability as the authority for the one record (or read-only listing) it covers, after the request ceiling, the tenant-scope check and the CD-10 registered-tenant / revoke-only content check, which now applies whenever the capability is active. Broadened, append- or name-prefix-enlarged lookalikes authorize nothing.
- **Discussion request receipts are authorized by an opaque grant, not by a dedicated role** — the receipt facade issues an opaque, synchronous, non-transferable grant (the same issuer mechanism as DOT results) over the two records of one request; forged, copied, escaped, expired and counterfeit-issuer scopes authorize nothing. The `discussion_request_store` role is gone (security policy, authority role index and definition, `authority.ts`, store catalog `legacy_roles`); tier-guard treats the active grant as the authority after the request ceiling and the tenant-scope check, including the structural mkdir of the tenant container. Who may write receipts is unchanged: before issuing, the facade applies the same role-assumption rule to the name `discussion_request_store`, which stays in Concierge's `may_assume` as a grant issuance right, so other surfaces still get `[ROLE_ASSUMPTION_DENIED]`. The facade binds the I/O to the selected tenant (caller keeps its own role, no organization) and refuses a caller bound to another tenant.
